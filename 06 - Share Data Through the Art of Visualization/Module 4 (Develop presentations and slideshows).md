## Key Takeaways (The art and science presentations)

- **Using a Strategic Framework** [[Case Study Present with framework]]
	- A framework guides your presentation by creating logical connections tied to the business task and metrics.
	- Understanding the business task early helps frame data in a way that makes it meaningful and relevant to the audience.
	
	- **Framing Presentations with Business Context**
		- Start presentations by clearly stating the business task and goals to set audience expectations.
		- Use narrative techniques and speaker notes to highlight key points and maintain focus on the business task throughout.
	
	- **Connecting Data to Business Metrics**
		- Explain the business metrics used to organize and interpret data, helping the audience understand the impact of findings.
		- Provide context for data visualizations by describing data sources, time periods, and measurement methods to clarify insights.

- **Understanding the Data Context**
	- Explain what data was available during collection and any new or missing data relevant to the analysis.
	- Use examples like online search data for "avocado" to clarify what questions the data can answer.
	
	- **Establishing Hypothesis and Using Visualizations**
		- State the initial hypothesis early to provide context for the data presentation.
		- Use clear examples and visualizations, such as graphs showing trends, to make raw data easier to understand.
	
	- **Applying the McCandless Method for Data Presentation** [[Case Study McCandless Method]]
		- Introduce the graphic by name to focus the audience's attention.
		- Answer obvious questions about the data source and how to read the visualization.
		- State key insights clearly before moving to supporting details.
		- Highlight specific data points to support insights.
		- Explain why the insights matter and their business impact, guiding stakeholders on actionable steps.

## Key Takeaways (Presentation skills and practices)

- **The role of a data analyst**
	- Data analysts have two main responsibilities: **analyzing data and effectively presenting their findings**.
	- Communicating insights clearly is essential because knowledge from data analysis only helps if it is shared well.
	
	- **Presentation fundamentals**
		- Effective presentations combine good visualizations, organized slides, and clear delivery.
		- Using frameworks like the McCandless Method helps structure presentations from broad context to specific insights.
	
	- **Tips for presenting data**
		- Manage nerves by taking deep breaths to channel excitement positively.
		- Use the five-second rule when showing data visualizations: pause to let the audience process, ask if they understand, explain if needed, then share conclusions.
		- Preparation is key, whether through rehearsals, scripting, or visualization techniques, to improve confidence and performance.

- **Audience Awareness and Accessibility**
	- Understand that your audience may not share your knowledge or context, so tailor your presentation to their needs and background.
	- Make your data visualizations accessible and share presentations thoughtfully, considering different ways your audience might experience them.
	
	- **Content Focus and Clarity**
		- Provide essential information such as data sources, collection methods, analysis goals, assumptions, and conclusions to help the audience follow your reasoning.
		- Avoid unnecessary details or unrelated stories that might distract the audience; keep the presentation concise and relevant.
	
	- **Presentation Delivery Best Practices**
		- Use clear, simple language with short sentences and intentional pauses to aid understanding.
		- Maintain good posture, purposeful movement, and positive eye contact; be mindful of nervous habits that could distract listeners.
		- Practice regularly and seek feedback to improve your presentation skills over time.

## Key Takeaways (Data caveats and limitations)

- **Understanding Stakeholder Expectations** [[Prepare for the Q&A]]
	- Know the project objectives and what stakeholders expect to gain from your presentation.
	- Align your preparation with these expectations to anticipate relevant questions.
	
	- **Identifying Possible Questions**
		- Conduct a "colleague test" by presenting to someone unfamiliar with your work to gather potential questions.
		- Use feedback to refine your presentation or prepare detailed answers for anticipated questions.
	
	- **Presentation Clarity and Team Coordination**
		- Avoid assuming the audience knows jargon or background information; explain key terms clearly.
		- Collaborate with your team to anticipate questions, draft responses, and incorporate different perspectives.
		- Be ready to discuss any limitations or potential weaknesses in your data and analysis methods.

- **Handle Objections**
	- **Objections about Data**
		- Stakeholders may question the source, transformations, freshness, and accuracy of the data.
		- Including data context at the start and detailed logs or appendices can help address these questions.
	
	- **Objections about Analysis**
		- Questions may arise about the reproducibility of the analysis and the steps taken.
		- Keeping a change log and clean scripts, and documenting feedback from others, supports transparency and credibility.
	
	- **Objections about Findings**
		- Stakeholders might ask if findings are consistent over time or if differences in data were controlled.
		- It is important to demonstrate that results are accurate, useful, and account for inconsistencies.
	
	- **Responding to Objections**
		- Communicate assumptions clearly, such as data cleaning processes.
		- Explain why analysis results might differ from expectations by walking through key variables.
		- Acknowledge valid objections and be willing to investigate further or follow up with more details.

- **Q&A Best Practice**
	- **Listening and Understanding Question**
		- Listen to the entire question before responding to ensure full understanding.
		- Repeat the question to confirm accuracy and give yourself time to prepare your answer.
	
	- **Context and Audience Engagement**
		- Understand the context and interests of your audience to tailor your answers accordingly.
		- Involve the whole audience in your response, not just the person who asked the question, and invite input from others if relevant.
	
	- **Answering Clearly and Concisely**
		- Keep answers short and to the point, starting with a clear headline response.
		- Use additional details only if the audience requests more information, and it’s acceptable to defer difficult questions for later follow-up.
