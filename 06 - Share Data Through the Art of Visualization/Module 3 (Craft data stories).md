## Key Takeaways (Data-driven storytelling)

- **The Role of Storytelling in Learning**
	- Storytelling has been used by humans for tens of thousands of years to convey knowledge, from cave paintings to modern narratives.
	- Stories help the brain organize information with a beginning, middle, and end, making it easier to process, remember, and relate to others.
	
	- **Evolution of Storytelling Tools**
		- Important inventions like the printing press, motion picture camera, and television have transformed how stories are told and shared.
		- Data visualization tools represent a modern evolution, enabling the presentation of data in ways that enhance understanding and engagement.
	
	- **Data Visualization and Storytelling in Analytics**
		- Data visualization involves representing data visually to aid comprehension and storytelling.
		- Dashboards consolidate multiple data sets into one place for tracking and analysis, while dashboard filters allow focusing on specific data criteria.
		- The course aims to teach how to use these tools to create compelling, data-driven stories that motivate action and communicate effectively in business contexts.

- **"Numbers have an important story to tell. They rely on you to give them a clear and convincing voice."** - Stephen Few

- **Data Storytelling**: communicating the meaning of a dataset with visuals and a narrative that are customized for each particular audience.
	
	- 3 data storytelling steps:
		1. **Engage your audience**: Engage your audience by capturing and holding their interest, considering who they are to tailor the story appropriately.
		
		2. **Create compelling visuals**: Create compelling visuals that clearly show data trends or highlight key insights, making the story easier to grasp.
		
		3. **Tell the story in an interesting narrative**: Tell the story with an interesting narrative that has a clear beginning, middle, and end, connecting data to objectives and insights.
		
	- **Examples and Techniques**
		- Examples include personalized year-in-review emails from music streaming services and ride-sharing companies showing environmental and cost benefits.
		- Word clouds are a simple visualization tool that highlights frequently occurring words to reveal themes in text data.

- **Speak to your audience** [[Case Study Speak to Your Audience]]
	- **Knowing Your Audience**
		- Understand the audience's role, stake, and expectations regarding the data project.
		- Consider how the data insights might impact different stakeholders, such as printing staff or magazine editors.
	
	- **Choosing Your Key Message**
		- Identify the most important insights by filtering out less relevant data.
		- Use techniques like spotlighting to highlight recurring themes or patterns that support the main message.
	
	- **Crafting and Delivering the Story**
		- Keep the key message clear and concise to maximize engagement.
		- Recognize that audience reactions may vary, but preparation increases the likelihood of successful communication.

- "You still have to uncover the meaning beneath it and really understand what's the insight within data."

## Key Takeaways (Tableau dashboards)

- **Tableau Dashboard Basics**
	- **Purpose and Function of Dashboards**
		- Dashboards organize information from multiple data sets into one central location for tracking, analysis, and visualization using tables, charts, and graphs.
		- They constantly monitor live incoming data, making it easier for users to understand and respond to key information quickly.
	
	- **Designing Effective Dashboards**
		- Dashboards should be tailored to the needs of specific stakeholders, focusing on the most important data points to avoid overwhelming users.
		- Layout and placement of visual elements like charts and graphs are crucial; options include vertical or horizontal layouts and tiled or floating items to create a balanced and clear visual.
	
	- **Sharing Dashboards and Collaboration**
		- Sharing dashboards allows stakeholders to explore data and draw their own conclusions, which can foster collaboration and innovation.
		- However, sharing also means losing control over the narrative, so it’s important to be aware of this when distributing dashboards.

- **Filters and Their Uses**
	- Filters allow you to show only data that meets specific criteria, hiding the rest, which helps tailor dashboards to stakeholder needs.
	- Filters can be applied to limit data by time, categories, or specific data points, and can be used to exclude or highlight outliers carefully.
	
	- **Creating Visuals with Spreadsheets and Tableau**
		- Spreadsheets like Google Sheets enable quick chart creation, including bar charts and maps, with customization options for colors and styles to improve accessibility.
		- Tableau dashboards can be prefiltered to save stakeholders time and focus their attention on the most relevant data, reducing clutter and enhancing clarity.
	
	- **Practical Application and Data Storytelling**
		- Filters help isolate important data segments, such as specific advertising tactics, making it easier to interpret and communicate insights.
		- Combining filters and charts supports building a clear narrative, which is essential for effective data storytelling and presentations.

## Key Takeaways (Share data stories)

- **Presentation Design and Themes**
	- A professional and organized slideshow helps maintain audience confidence and focus.
	- Themes control colors, fonts, formatting, and layout to create a consistent and appropriate tone for the presentation.
	
	- **Slide Content and Text**
		- Include a clear title, subtitle, and date to provide context.
		- Keep text concise (less than five lines and 25 words per slide) to avoid distracting the audience from the spoken message.
	
	- **Use of Visuals**
		- Visuals should clearly support the key message and be easy to understand without ambiguity.
		- Avoid overcrowding slides; use separate visuals for different points and highlight important elements with arrows or call-outs.
	
	- **Handling Visuals in Slideshows**
		- Copy-pasting visuals allows editing within the slideshow but does not update with changes in the original data.
		- Linking visuals keeps them updated automatically when the source changes.
		- Embedding visuals creates an independent copy that does not update with the source but can be edited separately.
	
	- **Practical Application**
		- Learners are encouraged to practice by creating slideshows with appropriate themes, text, visuals, and an engaging reveal.
		- Experimenting with pasting, linking, and embedding visuals helps understand their different behaviors in presentations.
