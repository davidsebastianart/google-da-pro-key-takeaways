## Key Takeaways (Data integrity and analytics objectives)

- **Data Integrity**: The accuracy, completeness, consistency, and trustworthiness of dat throughout its lifecycle. [[More about data integrity and compliance]]

	- Maintaining data integrity is crucial because compromised data can lead to incorrect analysis and potentially serious consequences.
	
	- **Risks to Data Integrity**
		- **Data replication**: the process of storing data in multiple locations. Data replication can cause inconsistencies if data is stored in multiple locations at different times.
		
		- **Data transfer**: the process of copying data from a storage device to memory, or from one computer to another. Data transfer interruptions may result in incomplete datasets.
		
		- **Data manipulation**: the process of changing data to make it more organized and easier to read. Errors during data manipulation can reduce the efficiency and accuracy of analysis.
		
		- Other risks include human error, viruses, malware, hacking, and system failures.

- It's important to check that the data you use aligns with the business objective. [[Well-aligned objectives and data]]

## Key Takeaways (Overcome the challenges of insufficient data)

- **Types of insufficient data**:
	- Data from only one source
	- Data that keeps updating
	- Outdated data
	- Geographically-limited data

- **Ways to address insufficient data**:
	- Identify trends with the available data
	- Wait for more data if time allows
	- Talk with stakeholders and adjust your adjective
	- Look for new dataset

- **Sample Size and Population** [[Calculate sample size]]
	- A population includes all possible data values in a dataset, but analyzing the entire population is often impractical due to time and cost.
	
	- A sample size is a representative subset of the population used to make predictions or conclusions about the whole population more efficiently.
	
	- Benefits and Challenges of Using Samples
		- Using a sample size is more cost-effective and faster than collecting data from the entire population.
		- However, samples can introduce uncertainty and may not perfectly represent the population, leading to sampling bias.
	
	- Addressing Sampling Bias
		- Sampling bias occurs when some members of the population are overrepresented or underrepresented in the sample.
		- Random sampling helps reduce bias by giving every member of the population an equal chance of being selected, ensuring a more representative sample.

## Key Takeaways (Test your data)

- **Statistical power**: the probability of getting meaningful results from a test.
	- "**Statistical power** can be calculated and reported for a completed experiment to comment on the confidence one might have in the conclusions drawn from the results of the study. It can also be used as a tool to estimate the number of observations or sample size required in order to detect an effect in an experiment."
	
	- If a test is statistically significant, it means the results of the test are real and not an error caused by random chance.
	
	- Usually, you need a statistical power of at least zero point 8 or 80% to consider your results statistically significant.

- **Hypothesis testing**: a way to see if a survey or experiment has meaningful results.

- **Confidence level**: the probability that your sample size accurately reflects the greater population. [[Sample size calculator]]
	- Having a 99% confidence level is ideal, but most industries hope for at least 90% or 95% percent confidence level.

## Key Takeaways (Consider the margin of error)

- **Margin of error**: the maximum amount that the sample results are expected to differ from those of the actual population. [[All about margin of error]]
	
	- **Practical Example and Interpretation**
		- For example, a survey showing 60% preference with a 10% margin of error means the true population preference could be between 50% and 70%.
		- A confidence level (e.g., 95%) indicates the probability that the population's true value falls within this margin.
	
	- **Calculating Margin of Error**
		- To calculate margin of error, **you need population size, sample size, and confidence level.**
		- Increasing sample size reduces margin of error, improving result reliability, while lowering confidence level also reduces margin but decreases accuracy.
		- Tools like spreadsheets and online calculators can assist in computing margin of error for your data analysis tasks.
